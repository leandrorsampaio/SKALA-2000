A red pushbutton under a hinged red guard inside a hazard-striped frame, for a disruptive command, with a key switch beside it when the command cannot be undone. There is one, F12 END SESSION, alone under DISRUPTIVE COMMANDS at the foot of panel B, acting on the selected session. The unkeyed F10 and F11 had no job and are gone; the component still draws a guard without a key.

**Three deliberate acts.**
1. *Lift the guard* (the guard is plain translucent red acrylic with no lettering; the hazard stripes already say what it is): click the flap; it swings up toward the operator about a steel hinge rod, overshoots to 121° and rests at 112°, 260 ms, with a `clunk`. Lifted, it stays clickable.
   *Lower the guard*: click the lifted flap, or wait 5 s after the last interaction, or a command is confirmed. It is not driven shut, it **falls**: accelerating from 112° to 0 in 270 ms, a `clunk` as it lands, a bounce to 15°, a second landing with a `click`, a last 4° flutter, at rest after 520 ms.
2. *Key (F12)*: click the key switch; it turns 90° to ARMED with a `clunk`. It stays armed until turned back. Without it the button travels but nothing is sent.
3. *Press and hold 2 s*: the button feeds a time-delay relay. **Nothing on the panel shows the hold: no bar, no ring, no countdown**; a 1972 desk has no way to draw one. While held, the cap simply stays sunk in its hole. Releasing early drops the relay out: nothing is sent and nothing indicates it. At 2 s the relay pulls in with a `clunk` (the only cue that the hold was long enough) and the command is sent; on confirmation the cap glows for 1.5 s, then the guard falls shut.

The closed guard covers the button completely: it cannot be pressed, focused or triggered from the keyboard until the guard is open. There is no keyboard shortcut for these functions, ever.

**Consumer provides:** cap text, plate label, whether a key is required, the command, the confirmation signal.

**Native.** Guard = a layer rotated about its top edge with perspective (m34 = −1/400). Log every guard lift, key turn and confirmed command with a timestamp.

**Depth.** The red frame is a raised cast collar: 5px wide, bevelled (light red top and left, dark red right and bottom), a thin dark outline, a drop shadow onto the panel, and an inner shadow falling onto the hazard stripes so the well reads as sunk inside the collar. The hinge rod lies across the collar's top edge.
